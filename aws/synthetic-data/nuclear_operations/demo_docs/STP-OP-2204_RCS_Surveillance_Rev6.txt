SECTION 1.  PURPOSE & SCOPE
================================================================================

1.1  PURPOSE.  This standard establishes the surveillance frequency, acceptance
     criteria, and corrective actions for vibration monitoring of the four (4)
     Reactor Coolant Pumps (RCPs) at South Texas Project Units 1 and 2,
     consistent with Technical Specification Limiting Condition for Operation
     (LCO) 3.4.6 and Surveillance Requirement (SR) 3.4.6.1.

1.2  SCOPE.  This procedure applies to:

     (a) Continuous vibration monitoring per RCP via the Bently Nevada
         3500 Series machinery monitoring system;
     (b) Quarterly walk-down vibration surveillance using the Emerson
         CSI 2140 portable analyzer;
     (c) Lockout/tagout (LOTO) provisions when an RCP is removed from
         service for maintenance;
     (d) Mechanical seal leakoff measurement; and
     (e) Reactor coolant pump motor lubricating-oil sampling.

1.3  EXCLUSIONS.  This standard does NOT cover:

     (a) Component cooling water (CCW) pump surveillance — see STP-OP-2306;
     (b) Auxiliary feedwater pump surveillance — see STP-OP-2412;
     (c) Charging pump surveillance — see STP-OP-2118.

SECTION 2.  DEFINITIONS
================================================================================

  ACTION STATEMENT — A specific Tech Spec requirement triggered when an
  LCO becomes inoperable. Action statements impose a completion time
  during which the plant must be returned to compliance or shut down.

  CRITICAL VIBRATION LIMIT — A peak velocity reading at which an RCP
  trip is automatically initiated to protect the pump and the RCS
  pressure boundary. Set at 0.50 in/s per ANSI 12.10 Class IIA pumps.

  ALERT VIBRATION LIMIT — A peak velocity reading at which Tech Spec
  entry is required within 4 hours and operations must enter ACTION
  Statement A of LCO 3.4.6. Set at 0.30 in/s per axis.

  DEGRADED OPERATION — RCP operation between the alert limit and the
  critical limit. Continued operation requires Engineering evaluation
  per ED-FORM-014 and ODMP entry per STP-415-A.

  BENTLY NEVADA 3500 — The plant's permanently-installed continuous
  vibration monitoring system. Provides 1× and 2× spectra, overall
  velocity, axial position, and bearing temperature.

  EMERSON CSI 2140 — The portable vibration analyzer used for
  quarterly walk-down surveillance, route data trending, and root-cause
  spectral analysis when a continuous-system alarm is received.

SECTION 3.  ROLES, RESPONSIBILITIES, AND QUALIFICATIONS
================================================================================

3.1  SHIFT SUPERVISOR.  Authorizes RCP downpower per Section 10 when an
     LCO action statement is entered.

3.2  REACTOR OPERATOR (RO).  Monitors continuous vibration trends on
     panel CRT-RCS-04. Notifies the Shift Supervisor upon receipt of
     an alert alarm.

3.3  PREDICTIVE MAINTENANCE ANALYST.  Performs quarterly route data
     collection, performs FFT spectrum analysis, generates trend
     reports per PMA-REC-002.

3.4  MECHANICAL ENGINEER.  Reviews continuous-monitor alarms and
     quarterly trend reports. Recommends corrective maintenance.

3.5  QUALIFICATIONS.  Personnel performing surveillance under this
     procedure shall hold:

     - SRO/RO license for monitoring activities (Section 5)
     - Predictive Maintenance Analyst Certification per PMP-CERT-001
       for spectral analysis (Section 6, 8, 9)
     - LOTO Authorized Worker per SAFE-PROC-301 for lockout (Section 7)

SECTION 4.  SURVEILLANCE FREQUENCIES & ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
================================================================================

4.1  CONTINUOUS MONITORING.  All four (4) RCPs shall have continuous
     vibration monitoring active whenever the RCS is at operating
     pressure (≥ 2,235 psia) and the pump is operating.

4.2  QUARTERLY WALK-DOWN.  A complete vibration surveillance route
     shall be performed every 92 days, ± 25%, per SR 3.4.6.1.

4.3  ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — VIBRATION VELOCITY (PEAK).

     Per axis (X, Y, Z), measured at the upper bearing housing:

       NORMAL OPERATION  : ≤ 0.20 in/s
       WATCH BAND        : 0.21 – 0.29 in/s
       ALERT LIMIT       : 0.30 – 0.49 in/s     (LCO entry within 4 hr)
       CRITICAL LIMIT    : ≥ 0.50 in/s          (Auto-trip)

4.4  ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — BEARING TEMPERATURE.

       NORMAL            : ≤ 175 °F
       WATCH             : 176 – 195 °F
       ALERT             : 196 – 219 °F          (LCO entry within 4 hr)
       CRITICAL          : ≥ 220 °F               (Auto-trip)

4.5  ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — MECHANICAL SEAL LEAKOFF.

       NORMAL            : ≤ 1.5 gpm
       WATCH             : 1.6 – 2.4 gpm
       ALERT             : 2.5 – 4.0 gpm          (LCO entry within 4 hr)
       CRITICAL          : ≥ 4.1 gpm              (Pump trip required)

SECTION 5.  VIBRATION MONITORING — CONTINUOUS
================================================================================

5.1  Reactor coolant pump vibration shall be monitored continuously by the
     Bently Nevada 3500 Series machinery monitoring system. Single-axis
     velocity readings exceeding 0.30 in/s peak shall trigger a Tech-Spec
     entry within 4 hours.

5.2  ALARM RESPONSE FLOW.

     Upon receipt of a "VIB-HI" alarm at the main control board:

     (a) The Reactor Operator shall:
         (i)   Verify the alarm is not spurious by cross-checking
               the redundant accelerometer (channel B);
         (ii)  Record the time, pump id, axis, and reading on
               Operational Log STP-LOG-OPS-DAILY;
         (iii) Notify the Shift Supervisor within 5 minutes.

     (b) The Shift Supervisor shall:
         (i)   Verify the alarm via independent observation of the
               Bently Nevada workstation in the control room;
         (ii)  If verified, enter LCO 3.4.6 ACTION A and initiate
               Operational Decision-Making Process (ODMP) per STP-415-A;
         (iii) Initiate work order request for vibration analysis
               using the Emerson CSI 2140 portable analyzer.

5.3  AUTOMATIC PROTECTIVE ACTIONS.  At ≥ 0.50 in/s, the pump trip relay
     activates automatically. No operator action shall override this
     protective signal except by the Shift Supervisor with concurrence
     of the Plant Manager.

SECTION 6.  VIBRATION SURVEILLANCE — QUARTERLY (SR 3.4.6.1)
================================================================================

6.1  FREQUENCY.  Every 92 days, ± 25%, per Tech Spec SR 3.4.6.1.

6.2  EQUIPMENT REQUIRED.

     - Emerson CSI 2140 portable vibration analyzer (calibration
       current within ± 30 days)
     - 622B01 ICP accelerometer (sensitivity 100 mV/g)
     - Magnetic mounting base, model 080A106
     - RCP route file (STP-RCP-ROUTE-2024)

6.3  PROCEDURE.

     (a) Pre-job briefing per OSHA 1910.147 and SAFE-PROC-301.
     (b) Verify pump operating at full design flow (≥ 88,500 gpm)
         and pressure (2,235 psia).
     (c) Collect data at the four (4) measurement points per pump:
         POINT-01  : Motor outboard bearing (vertical axis)
         POINT-02  : Motor outboard bearing (horizontal axis)
         POINT-03  : Pump-end bearing (vertical axis)
         POINT-04  : Pump-end bearing (horizontal axis)
     (d) Record overall velocity (in/s peak) and 1× spectral peak.
     (e) Trend against the most-recent 4 quarters of data.
     (f) Sign off the route in the eAM system by 17:00 on the day
         of collection.

6.4  ACCEPTANCE.  All four points must read within the WATCH BAND
     (≤ 0.29 in/s) for the surveillance to be marked SAT.

SECTION 7.  LOCKOUT / TAGOUT (LOTO) — PUMP MAINTENANCE
================================================================================

7.1  Prior to maintenance on any RCP, two-person verification of breaker
     open and tag-applied shall be documented in the work package per
     OSHA 1910.147 and SAFE-PROC-301.

7.2  LOCKOUT VERIFICATION CHECKLIST (signed by both Authorized Worker
     and an independent Verifier):

     [ ]  Breaker BKR-RCP-1A (or applicable) racked OUT
     [ ]  Personal lock applied — serial number recorded
     [ ]  Personal danger tag applied — serial number recorded
     [ ]  Try-test verification of zero energy state
     [ ]  Independent verification by Verifier (printed name, signature)

7.3  RE-ENERGIZATION.  Upon completion of work, locks/tags shall be
     removed only by the personnel who applied them. The Authorized
     Worker shall:

     (a) Walk down the work area and confirm no personnel remain in
         the LOTO boundary;
     (b) Remove personal lock and tag;
     (c) Notify the Shift Supervisor of LOTO release;
     (d) Document the release in the eAM work order with a timestamp.

7.4  PROCEDURE VIOLATIONS.  Any deviation from this section shall be
     reported to the Shift Supervisor and Industrial Safety per
     SAFE-REC-005 within 1 hour. Failure to perform two-person
     verification is a NRC-reportable event.

SECTION 8.  BEARING CONDITION MONITORING
================================================================================

8.1  Bearing condition shall be assessed using:

     (a) Bently Nevada 3500/72 (continuous) — monitors thrust position,
         radial vibration, bearing temperature.
     (b) Acoustic emission probes (continuous) — monitors high-frequency
         spike energy indicative of incipient bearing damage.
     (c) Quarterly route data per Section 6 — bearing-pass-frequency
         analysis.

8.2  TREND CRITERIA.  A 30% rise in spike energy across 3 consecutive
     quarterly walk-downs shall trigger:

     (a) Engineering evaluation per ED-FORM-022;
     (b) Submission of a Predictive Maintenance Action Recommendation
         (PMAR) form;
     (c) Consideration of advancing the next preventive maintenance
         cycle (PM-7B) per STP-MNT-1102.

SECTION 9.  LUBRICATING OIL SAMPLING AND ANALYSIS
================================================================================

9.1  FREQUENCY.  RCP motor lubricating oil samples shall be collected
     monthly during normal operation, and within 24 hours of any
     vibration or bearing-temperature alert.

9.2  SAMPLE VOLUME.  150 mL per sample, collected from the bottom
     drain after the motor has been at operating temperature for
     ≥ 4 hours.

9.3  LAB ANALYSIS — STP CHEMISTRY LABORATORY.

     The following parameters shall be reported within 72 hours:

     - Iron (Fe)         : ≤ 50  ppm (ALERT > 50)
     - Copper (Cu)       : ≤ 25  ppm (ALERT > 25)
     - Lead (Pb)         : ≤ 30  ppm (ALERT > 30)
     - Tin (Sn)          : ≤ 20  ppm (ALERT > 20)
     - Water content     : ≤ 100 ppm (ALERT > 100)
     - Total acid number : ≤ 1.5 mg KOH/g (ALERT > 1.5)

9.4  ALERT TREND.  Two (2) consecutive monthly samples showing wear
     metals above the alert limit shall trigger PMAR submission and
     consideration of advancing the bearing inspection PM.


================================================================================
SECTION 10. TECH SPEC ACTION STATEMENTS
================================================================================

10.1 LCO 3.4.6 — REACTOR COOLANT PUMP OPERABILITY.

     APPLICABILITY: MODES 1, 2, 3.

     ACTION A — One RCP inoperable:
       A.1  Restore the inoperable RCP to OPERABLE within 7 days, or
       A.2  Be in MODE 4 within the following 12 hours.

     ACTION B — Two or more RCPs inoperable:
       B.1  Be in MODE 3 within 6 hours, AND
       B.2  Be in MODE 4 within the following 12 hours.

10.2 ENTRY CRITERIA.  An RCP shall be declared inoperable upon:

     (a) Vibration in any single axis ≥ 0.30 in/s sustained for > 4 hr
     (b) Bearing temperature ≥ 196 °F sustained for > 4 hr
     (c) Mechanical seal leakoff ≥ 2.5 gpm sustained for > 4 hr
     (d) Two consecutive monthly oil samples with wear metals above
         alert limits (Section 9.4)

10.3 ODMP REQUIREMENT.  ANY entry into LCO 3.4.6 ACTION A shall
     additionally trigger an Operational Decision-Making Process
     review per STP-415-A (Operational Decision-Making Process)
     within 24 hours of LCO entry.
